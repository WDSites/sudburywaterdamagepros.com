The first 24 to 72 hours, in order

Mitigation follows a fixed order because each step protects the next one. A crew cannot dry a room while a pipe is still leaking, and drying equipment is wasted if soaked pad is left in place.

Here is the sequence most Sudbury jobs follow once we arrive.

  • Shut-off. Find and close the main valve or the fixture stop. A burst pipe or failed washer hose can release several gallons a minute.
  • Extraction. Extraction units and submersible pumps remove standing water first. Every gallon pulled out is a gallon the dehumidifiers never have to handle.
  • Moisture mapping. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show which walls, subfloors and ceilings are wet, so drying is aimed at real wet spots.
  • Drying setup. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in, and wet materials that cannot be saved come out.
  • Monitoring. Readings are taken again every day until the numbers match dry reference points.

How structural drying actually works

Structural drying is the part most homeowners never see. Water does not stay where it spilled. It wicks up drywall, soaks into the bottom plate of a wall, and slips under flooring into the subfloor. Fans alone move air across the surface but leave the wet core of lumber and gypsum untouched.

Proper drying works as a closed system. Doors and windows stay shut so the room air can be controlled. Air movers blow dry air across wet surfaces in a pattern and lift moisture off them. LGR dehumidifiers then pull that moisture out of the air and drain it away, so the air can absorb more.

Where water is trapped inside a wall cavity, small holes are sometimes drilled so warm dry air can flow in. Under hardwood or tile, mats can draw moisture from the subfloor. The crew adjusts equipment each day as readings fall. Most structures need about three to five days, but heavy materials like plaster, thick framing or soaked insulation can take longer.

The equipment in plain words

Here is what each machine does, so the noise and the cords make sense.

  • Air movers are low, wide fans. They push air along walls and floors so water at the surface evaporates faster.
  • LGR dehumidifiers are low grain refrigerant units. They keep working even when the air is already fairly dry, which is why they are used instead of a household dehumidifier.
  • Moisture meters read how wet drywall, wood and concrete are. Pin meters touch the material and scanning meters read through it.
  • A thermal imaging camera shows cooler wet patches on walls and ceilings. It finds the area to check, and the moisture meter confirms it.
  • Extraction units and submersible pumps remove standing water from carpet, tile and basements.

Why water lingers in Sudbury homes

The Sudbury River forms the southeast edge of town, and Hop Brook runs from the Wayside Inn area through the King Philip district into the river. Homes near these low areas can see wet basements and soggy crawl spaces after heavy spring runoff. If your lot sits near the river or the brook, check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Most homes in town also use a septic tank and leach field rather than a sewer main. If water backs up from a drain or the yard is saturated, treat it as possible Category 3 water and call before touching it. After a loss, ask the Town of Sudbury Building Department before rebuilding framing, electrical or plumbing, since permits may apply.

Drying goals under the S500 standard

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published guide the trade follows for water damage work. In general terms, it asks for drying goals instead of a fixed number of days. A crew sets target moisture readings from similar unaffected materials in your home, then dries until the wet materials reach them.

This is why a job ends when readings say so, not when a calendar says so. S500 also classes the loss by how much water the materials absorbed and sorts it into Category 1, 2 or 3 by contamination level. Those two ideas drive how much equipment goes in and what must be removed.

Where mitigation stops

Mitigation is not the rebuild. Once the structure is dry, replacing drywall, insulation, flooring and paint is a separate job covered under water damage repair. It is also not plumbing repair, so a leaking supply line still needs a plumber to fix it.

If standing water is deep, emergency water removal comes first. When wet materials need to be torn out and the area sanitized, see water damage cleanup. Lingering moisture can also lead to mold, which is handled under mold remediation.

Not waterproofing

Drying a structure does not make it waterproof. If water keeps entering from outside, the entry point still needs a fix.

What affects the cost of mitigation

Mitigation cost depends on how much of the building got wet and how long the water sat. Two jobs with the same leak can differ a lot in scope.

  • The water category matters, since Category 3 water means more removal and more protection for the crew.
  • The size of the affected area decides how many air movers and dehumidifiers are needed.
  • Materials change the time, because plaster and thick framing hold water longer than drywall.
  • Drying days and equipment use add up, since units run around the clock until readings drop.
  • Rebuild scope is separate, so tear-out and repair are priced on their own.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and a call to the 24/7 line is the fastest way to get a real number for your situation.

Mitigation and your insurance policy

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs a separate flood policy. Sump pump failure and water backup may require an added endorsement.

Check your policy or ask your insurer before you decide what to expect. Take photos of the damage and keep records of what you remove, since those notes help any claim conversation.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water mitigation emergencies at night or on weekends in Sudbury?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays. Same-day response is offered when available, and the dispatcher will ask a few questions about the source and the amount of water so the right equipment goes on the truck.

Is my wall wet inside even though it looks fine?

It can be. Drywall hides moisture inside the cavity, and paint may look normal for days. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera can check behind the surface without cutting into the wall first.

How long does structural drying take?

Most jobs run about three to five days, but that is a general range and not a promise. Dense materials, hardwood, plaster and wet insulation take longer. The daily readings decide when equipment can leave.

Why not just run box fans and a household dehumidifier?

Box fans move air but do not control humidity, and a household unit is too small for a whole wet room. Pulling moisture from framing and subfloors takes air movers and LGR dehumidifiers working together.

Do you rent air movers and dehumidifiers?

No. We do not offer equipment rentals. Drying is run and monitored by our crew because the placement and daily adjustments matter as much as the machines.

What is a thermal imaging camera used for?

It shows temperature differences on a surface. Wet areas often read cooler, so the camera points the crew to where to measure. The moisture meter then gives the actual reading.

Can I stay in the house during drying?

Often yes, but the equipment is loud and runs all day. If the water was Category 3 or the power is off in affected rooms, you may need to stay elsewhere. The crew will tell you what is safe once they see the layout.
