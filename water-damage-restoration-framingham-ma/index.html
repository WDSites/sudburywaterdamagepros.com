Saxonville, where the river and the old mills meet

Saxonville grew up where Concord Street, Water Street and Central Street meet, and the Sudbury River powered its mills. The former Roxbury Carpet Company complex is now an industrial park and a historic district. Brick and wood floors in older buildings hold moisture long after the standing water is gone. We check walls with moisture meters, not just floors.

The National Weather Service flood stage for the Sudbury River at Saxonville is 10 feet. The river reached 10.2 feet on Nov 13, 2018, and it spilled over its banks in parts of the village again on Jan 10, 2024. Ask your insurer about a separate flood policy, because flood is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. Our flood damage restoration crew treats river water as Category 3, and the same river runs upstream through Wayland.

Homes near Saxonville Beach on the northwest shore of Lake Cochituate face a quieter problem, which is rain with nowhere to go. Make sure downspouts empty well away from the foundation before the next storm.

Nobscot and the slopes around Hemenway Road

Nobscot village sits where Water Street, Edmands Road and Edgell Road meet, close to Nobscot Hill. That hill's north slope is the highest ground in Sudbury, so our home base shares the ridge with you. Houses on a slope shed rain toward the lower lots, and basement seepage often shows up on the wall facing uphill.

After a hard storm, look for a dark stripe along the bottom of that wall and check the carpet edge for dampness. Tell us which wall is wet when you call, because it points us to the entry path.

Wooded lots such as those off Hemenway Road drop leaves into gutters all fall. A clogged gutter can send a roof's worth of water down one corner of the house. Our water mitigation team maps moisture along that corner first and sets air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to match.

Framingham Centre and the lowered Route 9 corridor

Framingham Centre sits where Route 9, Route 30, High Street, Main Street and Edgell Road come together, with Framingham State University close by. The Centre Common Historic District is the old heart of the area. If your home or building is inside it, ask the building department what applies before you rebuild.

In the late 1960s an overpass lowered Route 9 below the local roads. Low pavement collects runoff in a cloudburst, so garages and basements nearby can take water fast. If a garage floor drain backs up, stop using water in the house and call us.

Once the building is dry, water damage repair covers the drywall, insulation and flooring that had to come out.

South Framingham, the railroad and the factory sites

South Framingham became the commercial center after the railroad arrived in the 1880s. Dennison Manufacturing and the former GM Framingham Assembly plant, closed in the late 1980s, were part of that story. Dense blocks of storefronts and apartments share walls, floors and pipes, so one leak can wet three units. Tell us every unit that shows a stain and tell the landlord the same day.

Farm Pond lies in this part of the city. After a long rain, groundwater near open water can rise, and a sump pump that never stops running is a warning sign. Test the pump before storms. Check your declarations pages or ask your agent about a water backup endorsement, since sump pump failure is often covered only by a rider.

Before we arrive at your Framingham property

A few quick steps protect your belongings and keep everyone safe while you wait for the crew.

Find the source

Close the main valve or the shut-off under the sink, toilet or water heater. If the water is not clean, stay out of it.

Power down with care

Turn off breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise leave it for us.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes, laptops and framed photos onto counters or upper floors. Leave heavy furniture alone.

Keep people and pets clear

Children and animals should stay out of standing water, especially after a sewer backup or a river rise.

Call the 24/7 line

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Framingham?

Yes. We serve both 01701 and 01702, from Saxonville and Nobscot in the north to Framingham Centre and South Framingham near Farm Pond. If you are unsure which ZIP applies to your street, give us the nearest cross streets on the call.

Is emergency water removal available in Framingham?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered at any hour, and we dispatch from Sudbury, about 6 miles north of the city line. We discuss timing on the call once we know the roads, the water depth and the type of loss.

What should I do after a sewer backup in my basement?

Stay out of the water and keep pets away. Sewage is Category 3, which means it can carry contaminants, so cleanup needs protective gear, extraction and disinfection. Call us and report the backup to the city's Wastewater Division of Public Works if the problem may come from the sewer main.

Does my homeowners policy cover Sudbury River flooding?

Usually not. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood damage, and flood needs a separate policy. Ask your agent, and if you do have flood coverage, report the claim right away.

How long does structural drying take?

Most jobs take several days, and thick framing or brick takes longer. We take moisture readings every day and stop equipment only when the materials reach their dry standard under ANSI/IICRC S500.

Do you handle apartment buildings and storefronts in South Framingham?

Yes. Shared walls and floors mean we check neighboring units for hidden moisture, and we coordinate access with the property manager. Our water damage cleanup service removes soaked materials unit by unit.

Can you help with mold after a leak near Framingham Centre?

Yes. Mold needs moisture, so we dry the structure first, then set containment barriers and HEPA air scrubbers under ANSI/IICRC S520. See our mold remediation page, and ask any company whether state or local rules apply. Bring health questions to your physician.
