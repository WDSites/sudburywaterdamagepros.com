Shoe-factory streets and the Marlborough Center Historic District

Samuel Boyd opened Marlborough's first shoe factory in 1836, and shoemaking drove the economy for decades. The town became a city in 1890. Downtown buildings from that era often have plaster walls, wood floors and layers of later repairs, and the Marlborough Center Historic District is centered on Main Street.

Plaster holds water like a sponge and dries slowly. We scan with a thermal imaging camera, confirm with moisture meters, and open walls only where the readings call for it. If your building sits inside the district, ask the building department what applies before you replace trim, windows or original features.

After the structure is dry, our water damage repair team can match plaster, flooring and paint to what was there.

Highway-era buildings along Route 20 and Route 85

Marlborough shifted from shoes to high-tech and electronics once I-495, I-290 and the Mass Pike were in place. Newer buildings from that growth tend to use drywall, carpet and suspended ceilings, which soak up water quickly and can be cut out and replaced faster than plaster.

Route 20 runs through the city as Boston Post Road, East and West Main Street, Lakeside Avenue and Granger Boulevard. Route 85 appears as Washington Street, Bolton Street and Maple Street. Tell us the name on your street sign, not the route number, because that is how our crew finds the right driveway.

Southwest Quadrant offices and business losses

The Southwest Quadrant is a revitalized area of office parks along Forest Street, Cedar Hill Street and nearby roads by I-495. A broken sprinkler line, a failed roof drain or an overflowing HVAC condensate pan can soak a ceiling grid over a weekend with nobody there to notice.

For a tenant, the first calls are to the building manager and to us. We shut off the affected zone, extract water, lift wet ceiling tile and carpet, and set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers so staff can return to dry rooms. Our water damage cleanup crew works around your hours when we can.

Lake Williams, Millham Reservoir and Fort Meadow

Three large lakes and reservoirs sit inside city limits: Lake Williams, Millham Reservoir and Fort Meadow Reservoir. Part of Fort Meadow reaches into Hudson. The Assabet River also crosses the northwest corner of the city.

Homes on low shoreline lots can see groundwater rise after long storms, and a basement that stays damp for weeks invites mold. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage, since a standard homeowners policy leaves flood out. Do this first, before our crew reaches Marlborough

These five actions limit the spread whether the loss is a kitchen floor or a whole office suite.

Shut the supply

Turn off the main valve or the nearest fixture valve. In an office, ask the building manager to close the zone valve.

Be careful with power

Cut breakers to wet areas only from dry ground. If water is dripping through light fixtures, leave the room.

Clear the floor

Pick up files, laptops, rugs and stock from the floor. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs on wet carpet.

Avoid contaminated water

If the water came from a drain or toilet, keep everyone out. Do not run fans to move it around.

Call and describe the loss

Use the 24/7 line. Give us the street name, the floor and what the walls and floors are made of.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Marlborough?

Is emergency water removal available in Marlborough?

Do you work in commercial buildings?

Yes. We handle offices, shops and multi-tenant buildings, and we coordinate with the property manager on access, shut-off valves and after-hours work. Commercial drying follows the same ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Is plaster harder to dry than drywall?

Usually. Plaster over lath holds moisture and has dead air space behind it. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to find the wet pockets, then dry in place when we can instead of tearing out sound plaster.

What if the loss reaches a neighboring unit?

Tell us right away. We check shared walls and ceilings for hidden moisture and report what we find to you in writing so you can share it with the owner and your insurer.

Can mold form after a lake-side basement stays damp?

Mold needs moisture, so a basement that stays damp can develop it. Our mold remediation process follows ANSI/IICRC S520, and for health questions you should ask your physician. We cannot promise mold will never return, so we fix the moisture source too.
