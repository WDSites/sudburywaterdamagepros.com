Burst Pipe Water Damage in a Sudbury, MA Winter: What to Do First

A split pipe can put hundreds of gallons into a house in a few hours, so the first minutes count.

Call (978) 252-8637

By the Sudbury Water Damage Pros team. Updated October 29, 2026.

Burst pipe water damage in Sudbury, MA usually follows a hard freeze, when water inside a pipe turns to ice, expands and splits the metal or plastic. The pipe often fails at the ice blockage, but the water only runs once the ice thaws, so the flood can begin hours after the cold snap ends. A single split in a half-inch line can release a lot of water in a short time.

New England winters make this a seasonal risk for any house with pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, garages or attics. This guide covers the first minutes, how to tell a frozen pipe from a burst one, and how the drying work runs once the water is stopped.

The first five minutes

Shut off the water at the main valve first. In most houses it sits where the supply line enters, often in the basement or utility room, and it turns clockwise to close. Learn where yours is before winter, because looking for it in a flooded basement is a bad time.

Then think about electricity. If water is dripping through a ceiling light, pooling around outlets or reaching a panel, stay out of the wet area and shut off power only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. If you cannot, call the 24/7 line and wait outside the wet zone.

Power and water do not mix

Never touch a switch, appliance or cord while standing in water. If the breaker panel is in a wet basement, leave it alone and ask for help.

Frozen pipe or burst pipe?

A frozen pipe has ice inside but no break yet. You will see a faucet that runs a trickle or nothing, and sometimes frost on the pipe's outside. A burst pipe has a split, and water may drip from the ceiling, stain a wall or run across a floor.

If a pipe is frozen but not leaking, open the faucet, and warm the area gently with a hair dryer on low. Never use an open flame. If you suspect a split, close the main valve before thawing anything, since thawing a cracked pipe is when the water starts flowing.

Where winter pipes tend to fail

Pipes in exterior walls are the usual suspects, especially on the north side where little sun reaches them. Kitchen and bath supply lines running through a cold cabinet wall are common. So are lines in unheated garages, crawl spaces, attics and outdoor spigots.

The burst can be hidden behind drywall. A stain that grows on a ceiling or wall, a hissing sound, or a water meter that keeps spinning with every tap closed all suggest a hidden leak. Check the meter, then call.

Ice dams: a related winter source

Not every winter leak starts in a pipe. An ice dam forms when snow on the roof melts over a warm area, runs down, then refreezes at the cold eave. The ice backs up meltwater under the shingles and into the attic, soaking insulation and ceilings.

Signs include icicles along the eave, brown stains near an exterior wall's top edge, and damp attic insulation. Water from an ice dam travels down through walls like a pipe leak. Rebuild work afterward falls under water damage repair, after the area is dried.

What category of water is it?

Category 1 water comes from a clean source, such as a broken supply line. Category 2 is gray water from a dishwasher or washing machine. Category 3 is grossly contaminated, like sewage or river water.

A burst supply line starts as Category 1, but time changes that. Left standing for more than 48 hours or soaked into insulation and carpet, clean water can degrade and the plan changes. Quick action keeps the job on the simpler end.

Document before you mop

Photograph and film the burst pipe, the standing water and every wet surface before cleaning up. Note the time you found it and the time you closed the valve. These records help if you file a claim.

Only your policy tells you what is covered. A split pipe is a sudden event, which insurers often treat differently from a slow drip. Ask your insurer what proof they want.

  • Take wide shots of each room, then close-ups of damage.
  • Keep the broken pipe section if a plumber removes it.
  • List damaged contents with approximate ages.
  • Save receipts for emergency repairs.

Extraction and drying, step by step

Once the water is off, crews pull standing water with extraction units, often truck-mounted or portable pumps. This is the core of emergency water removal. Soaked carpet pad, wet insulation and swollen drywall usually come out next.

Then the drying setup goes in. Air movers push air across wet surfaces, LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air, and meters plus a thermal imaging camera map what is still wet. Drying commonly takes three to five days, with readings checked daily, and equipment is removed only when materials return to dry targets. The work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

When to call the 24/7 line

Call as soon as the water is stopped, or sooner if you cannot stop it. Pipes do not burst on a schedule, and many go at night or on a holiday weekend. Same-day response is available when crews are free.

Call right away if water has reached ceilings, electrical panels or more than one room. Call if the house is cold and unoccupied, since a split pipe in a vacant house can run for days. Ask for the scope and price in that first call.

Local note

A Sudbury burst pipe: whose valve is whose

The Sudbury Water District is an independent municipal entity created by an Act of the Massachusetts Legislature in 1934, separate from the Town's Department of Public Works. If water is running from the street side of your meter rather than inside the house, that is a district matter, so call them. Anything inside your walls is yours.

When a plumber replaces pipe, the Town of Sudbury Building Department issues plumbing permits, so ask whether your repair needs one. Neighbors in Maynard and Marlborough face the same winter risks along Route 20 and the Assabet River.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do first if a pipe bursts?

Close the main water valve, then stay clear of any wet electrical area. Photograph the damage and call the 24/7 line.

Open a low faucet to drain remaining water from the lines.

How fast can a burst pipe flood a house?

A half-inch supply line under normal pressure can release hundreds of gallons in a few hours. Water finds its way through floors and into basements quickly.

That is why the main valve is the first step.

Does homeowners insurance cover burst pipes?

A sudden pipe failure is often covered, a long-running drip often is not, and flood is usually excluded.

Check your policy or ask your insurer, and ask about any freeze-related conditions.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

If it is not cracked, a hair dryer on low heat applied slowly works, with the faucet open. Never use a torch or open flame.

If you see a split or hear water, close the main valve and call instead.

Is an ice dam leak the same as a burst pipe?

The damage looks similar, but the source differs. Ice dam water comes through the roof and into attic insulation and ceilings.

Both need wet materials removed and dried. The roof cause also needs a roofer.

Who do I call in Sudbury if the water is leaking before my meter?

The Sudbury Water District runs the public supply and is separate from the Town's Department of Public Works. Leaks on the street side of your meter are its concern.

Inside the house, shut your main valve and call the 24/7 line for the water cleanup.
