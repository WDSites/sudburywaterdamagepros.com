Flood damage restoration is the work of removing storm, river or groundwater from a home and cleaning what it touched. If water is coming into your Sudbury, MA house now, the 24/7 line is open. While you wait, stop any water source you can safely reach, shut the main valve if a pipe is involved, and lift valuables off the floor.

Shut off power only when the panel is dry and easy to reach. Keep children and pets out of the water, since floodwater is rarely clean.

Sudbury River and Hop Brook flood exposure

The Sudbury River forms Sudbury's southeast boundary with Wayland, and Hop Brook runs from the Wayside Inn Historic District through the King Philip Historic District into the river. Both give water a path into low yards and basements when rain is heavy.

The river has a real flood record. On Nov 13, 2018, the National Weather Service warned that the Sudbury River at Saxonville reached 10.2 feet against a 10 foot flood stage, naming low-lying parts of Sudbury and Wayland. On Jan 10, 2024, the river was reported over its banks in parts of Saxonville and upstream in Wayland.

In March 2010, several Massachusetts river gauges set record crests, and the USGS published a report on the March 1968 flood of the Sudbury, Assabet and Concord Rivers.

The Wayland Historical Society notes that the river drops only a few inches from Heard Pond to Egg Rock. That flat gradient lets water back up instead of draining away. If your lot sits low or near the river or brook, photograph your basement and yard now so you know what normal looks like before the next storm.

Pumping and cleaning a flooded basement

A flooded basement is the most common flood call in a river town. Water comes in through floor cracks, window wells, the slab edge or a failed sump pump, and it often carries soil, oil and sewage with it.

We start by checking for electrical and gas hazards, then set submersible pumps and extraction units. Deep water comes down in stages, since emptying a basement too fast can let wet soil push on foundation walls. Once the floor is exposed, we remove soaked carpet, pad, drywall, insulation and stored cardboard.

Everything left is sanitized, because flood water is Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500. Then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while moisture meters track the concrete, sill plates and wall studs. Dry-out typically takes a matter of days, depending on materials. A basement that stays damp afterward may grow mold, which is a separate mold remediation job.

Sewage backup: stay out and call the right people first

Raw sewage is Category 3 water and carries bacteria and other contaminants. Stay out of the room. Do not wade in, run fans through it or try to mop it up yourself.

Safety comes first. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the panel, or you smell gas, get everyone out and phone the utility or 911 before the restoration line. We take the call once everyone is safe.

Most Sudbury homes are not on a town sewer main. The Town's wastewater plan treats onsite septic systems as the baseline, so a backup or a soaked yard can involve the tank and leach field. A septic service company may need to pump the tank before cleanup starts.

Our crew wears protective gear, extracts the liquid, removes porous materials like carpet and drywall that touched it, and applies disinfectant. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated before drying equipment goes in. Sewage jobs are never a do-it-yourself cleanup.

Crawl spaces and whole-house flooding

Crawl spaces flood quietly. Water sits under the floor for days, soaks the insulation and subfloor from below, and you may only notice a musty smell or a floor that feels soft. We pump the space, remove ruined insulation and vapor barrier, and dry the framing with air movers.

Whole-house flooding is a bigger job. Water that rose above the first floor reaches drywall, cabinets, flooring and appliances, and everything wet from Category 3 water is treated as contaminated. Contents are sorted into what can be cleaned and what must go. When the structure is dry, rebuilding is handled under water damage repair.

When flood water is rising inside your house

This is an emergency when water is still coming in, covers outlets, reaches the furnace or water heater, or smells like sewage. It is also an emergency when you cannot tell where it is coming from.

First, get everyone out of the water. If a pipe is the source, close the main valve. Do not use a wet-dry vacuum on deep water, and do not touch electrical items while standing in it. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk, then call the 24/7 line and we will talk through next steps.

  • Move people and pets to dry, higher ground.
  • Shut off the main valve only if water is from a pipe.
  • Leave the panel alone unless the floor around it is dry.
  • Photograph the water line and damaged items before moving them.

Water categories and what each one means

ANSI/IICRC S500 groups water by contamination level. The category decides which materials can be saved.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat happens to materials
Category 1Broken supply line, clean rainwater at the point of entryMost materials dried in place
Category 2Washing machine overflow, sump pump failure, dishwasher dischargeCarpet pad and soft materials usually removed, hard materials cleaned
Category 3River flooding, sewage backup, storm surge through the groundPorous materials removed and discarded, structure cleaned and disinfected

Flood and homeowners insurance

Per the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood damage, even away from coasts and rivers. Flood needs a separate policy, either through the National Flood Insurance Program or from a private insurer.

There is usually a 30-day waiting period before NFIP flood coverage takes effect, with no wait when it is bought with a loan closing or renewal. Sump pump failure or water backup may only be covered by an endorsement, so check your declarations pages or ask your agent. The Division's Consumer Service Unit handles coverage questions. If you do hold a flood policy, report the claim right away.

Check your address on the FEMA flood map

FEMA publishes flood maps that show how a property is classed for flood risk. Look up your address on the FEMA flood map and keep a copy with your insurance papers.

Many floods happen outside the mapped high-risk area, so a low-risk result does not mean zero risk. Ask your insurer or the building department what applies to your lot, and read about nearby river towns such as Framingham if you cross the river often.

How flood jobs are dried and sanitized

After water is out, we map moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera. Soaked drywall and insulation come out, hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected, and HEPA air scrubbers and containment barriers keep dust and spores off clean rooms.

Drying then follows the process described under water mitigation. Flood restoration is not waterproofing or sump repair, and it does not fix the cause of the flooding. A drainage or waterproofing contractor handles that.

What affects the price of a flood job

Flood work is priced by scope, not by the square foot, because Category 3 water changes what must be removed.

  • The water category, since river and sewage water means more materials come out and more sanitizing is needed.
  • The depth and area of water, which sets pump time and how many extraction units run.
  • The materials affected, such as carpet, drywall, insulation, subfloor and framing.
  • Drying days and equipment, including air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and air scrubbers.
  • The rebuild scope after drying, which may be a separate phase.

What to do about the claim

Standard homeowners policies usually leave flood out, so separate flood coverage matters. A sudden pipe break or appliance failure is a different situation, so ask your insurer which one applies.

Take photos and video before cleanup, list damaged items, and keep receipts. Check your policy and call your agent early.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle flood damage restoration emergencies at night or on weekends in Sudbury?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. We do not promise an arrival time, but we will tell you what to do while you wait and send a crew when available.

Is flood water the same as water from a burst pipe?

No. A burst clean-water pipe is usually Category 1. River and storm flood water is Category 3 under S500, which means more materials are discarded and surfaces are disinfected.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

We advise against it. Sewage is Category 3, and it soaks into drywall, carpet and wood. Call the utility or 911 first if power or gas is at risk, then call us.

My Sudbury house has a septic system. Does that change a backup?

It can. Most Sudbury homes use a septic tank and leach field. A backup may mean a full tank or a saturated leach field, so a septic service company should look at the system alongside cleanup.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Generally not. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says flood needs a separate policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer or agent.

How long does a flooded basement take to dry?

Drying is typically a matter of days, but concrete, framing and stored items can take longer. Moisture meters decide when drying is done, not the calendar.

Is a flooded home near the Sudbury River in Wayland handled the same way?

Yes. The river links both towns, and the same Category 3 process applies. The Wayland water damage restoration page covers the other bank.
