Sudbury and the towns around it

Sudbury Water Damage Pros is based at 339 Boston Post Road in Sudbury, on U.S. Route 20, the town's main commercial road. From there the crew reaches neighboring towns along Route 20, Route 27 and Route 117. No arrival time is promised, because traffic, weather and crew availability change.

Every town page uses that town's own geography. Rivers, mills, septic and sewer coverage, and the age of the buildings differ from place to place, and so do the first questions we ask on the phone.

Towns with their own pages

Framingham, about six miles south, covers ZIP codes 01701 and 01702 and the Saxonville stretch of the Sudbury River. Wayland, about five miles southeast across the river, covers 01778 and a floodplain with a long flood history.

Marlborough, about seven miles west, covers 01752 and the lake and reservoir shorelines. Maynard, about six miles north, covers 01754 and the Assabet River mill district, and Concord, about eight miles northeast, covers 01742 and the meeting of two rivers.

Nearby places we also answer for

Calls also come from Stow (01775), Hudson (01749), Weston (02493), Natick (01760) and Southborough (01772). These towns do not have separate pages yet, but the same 24/7 line applies. When you call, give the street address so the dispatcher can confirm the area.

If you live outside this list, call anyway. Coverage is decided case by case, and we will say so honestly if another company is closer. In every town, water work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and mold work follows S520.

Inside Sudbury

In Sudbury itself the work spans Sudbury Center, South Sudbury, the Wayside Inn area and the neighborhoods near Hop Brook and the Sudbury River. Foundations and basements vary from street to street, so the first questions on the phone are about how low your wet space is and where the water entered.

The Sudbury Water District was created by the Legislature in 1934, is separate from the Town, and names treatment plants on East Street and Raymond Road. Ask it about any main break or pressure problem, and ask the Town's Building Department about permits.

What to do before help arrives

Wherever you are, the first steps are the same. Stop the source if it is safe, stay out of water near outlets or panels, keep people and pets out of dirty water, and photograph the damage before moving things. Then call the 24/7 line.
