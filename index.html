Before the crew arrives

If water is spreading right now

These steps come before the phone call when they are safe to do. They are general guidance, not a promise about what a crew will find.

Stop the source

Shut the fixture valve, or the main water valve if you cannot find the source. In most houses the main shutoff sits where the water line enters the building.

Mind the electricity

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. If water touches outlets, panels or gas appliances, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

Lift what you can

Raise furniture legs onto foil or blocks, move rugs and electronics, and pull valuables off wet floors. Photograph everything before you move it.

Keep people out of dirty water

Gray water from appliances and black water from sewage or river flooding is contaminated. Keep children and pets away and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

Local conditions

Water damage in Sudbury, MA is shaped by the river, the soil and the septic tank

The Sudbury River forms the southeast boundary of town, and Hop Brook runs from the Wayside Inn Historic District through the King Philip Historic District into the river. The Wayland Historical Society notes the river drops only a few inches between Heard Pond and Egg Rock, so high water lingers. If your lot sits near Hop Brook or the river meadows, a basement can take on groundwater long after the rain stops.

Most Sudbury homes are not connected to a town sewer. The Town's wastewater planning page treats septic systems as the baseline, with three small treatment facilities at Avalon, Longfellow Glen and Lincoln-Sudbury High School. A backup in a house on septic can involve the tank or leach field, so the plumber and septic contractor matter as much as the cleanup crew, and the water counts as Category 3 until proven otherwise.

Older buildings around Sudbury Center and the Wayside Inn Historic District can have plaster and older framing that dry differently from newer drywall. Either way, ask the Sudbury Building Department before you rebuild framing, electrical or plumbing, because its page says permits are required for construction that is not an ordinary repair.

Know before you call

What drives the cost of water damage restoration

No honest quote exists before someone sees the loss. These five factors decide the scope, and the technician explains each one before work starts.

  • Water category: clean supply water, gray water from appliances, or black water from sewage and river flooding.
  • Affected area: the square footage and how many floors, rooms and wall cavities took on moisture.
  • Materials: carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, hardwood and plaster dry or fail at different rates.
  • Drying days and equipment: the number of air movers and dehumidifiers and how long readings take to reach dry standards.
  • Rebuild scope: whether the job ends at dry and clean or continues into drywall, flooring and paint.

Want a real number for your house? Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Insurance and water damage: what to check

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood damage and that flood needs its own policy. It also notes that sump pump failure or water backup may be covered only by an endorsement, so read your declarations pages or ask your agent.

We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and equipment logs so your insurer has records to review. We cannot promise a claim will be approved, and we do not bill insurers directly. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it requires.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Sudbury?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, including holidays, and the dispatcher will tell you what to do while you wait. We do not promise an arrival time, because it depends on conditions and crew availability, but same-day response is offered when available.

What should I do in the first hour after water damage?

Stop the source, make electrical safety your first concern, lift valuables, and photograph the damage before moving anything. Then call the 24/7 line. The water mitigation page explains what happens in the first 24 to 72 hours.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Massachusetts?

Often for sudden and accidental events, and often not for gradual leaks. Flood damage needs a separate flood policy, per the Massachusetts Division of Insurance. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and see our notes on flood damage restoration for river and basement flooding.

Which ZIP codes and towns do you serve?

We serve Sudbury (01776) and the nearby towns of Framingham (01701, 01702), Wayland (01778), Marlborough (01752), Maynard (01754) and Concord (01742), plus calls from Stow, Hudson, Weston, Natick and Southborough.

How long does drying take after a leak?

Drying is usually a matter of days and depends on the materials, the water category and the equipment. A technician reads moisture levels each visit and stops equipment only when the readings reach the drying goal. See water mitigation for the stages.

My Sudbury house is on septic. Does that change a sewage backup?

It can. The backup may come from the tank or leach field instead of a town sewer main, so a septic contractor may be needed alongside cleanup. Stay out of the water, treat it as contaminated, and call the 24/7 line for the cleanup side.

Is water damage restoration the same as basement waterproofing?

No. Restoration removes water, dries the structure and repairs damage. Waterproofing changes the foundation or drainage so water stays out, and that is a different trade. We will say so plainly if your problem needs a waterproofing contractor.
