Spring: snowmelt and the Assabet River

The Assabet River crosses Maynard from west to east, under seven road bridges and one foot bridge. It drops about 30 feet between the Stow border and the Acton border. Average flow is about 200 cubic feet per second, and in March 2010 the river reached about 2,500, more than twelve times the average.

Snowmelt, ice and debris jams are all recognized river-flood triggers, according to the National Weather Service. In early spring, check your sump pump, clear window wells and lift stored boxes off the basement floor. Our flood damage restoration crew treats river water as Category 3, so please keep children and pets out of it.

Neighboring Concord is where the Assabet joins the Sudbury River, and we serve both sides of that meeting.

Winter: frozen pipes in the Presidential district

The Presidential district on the south side was built in 1902 and 1903 for American Woolen workers. Its streets carry the names of eight post-Civil War presidents, from Arthur and Cleveland to Hayes and Roosevelt. Houses of that age often have pipes in exterior walls and crawl spaces, where cold air reaches them first.

Know where your main shut-off is before January. If a pipe freezes, close the main, open the nearest faucet and call us before the ice thaws, since the break often shows itself only when the water starts moving. Our emergency water removal team extracts the water, and the ceiling below gets a moisture check.

Summer: humidity in the mill buildings

The Assabet Woolen Mill was built in 1846 and 1847, ran as a woolen mill until 1950 and later housed Digital Equipment Corporation from 1957 to 1998. The complex is now Mill & Main Place, and its clock tower dates to 1892. Big masonry buildings take on humidity in July, and condensation can form on cold pipes and ducts.

Tenants should report a musty smell or a damp ceiling early. Run a dehumidifier, watch a hygrometer and have air conditioning drain lines cleared each season. When moisture lingers, mold remediation under ANSI/IICRC S520 begins with drying and containment barriers.

Fall: leaves, gutters and storms off Summer Hill

Summer Hill is Maynard's highest point at 358 feet, and the lowest ground is 145 feet where the Assabet leaves town. That is a drop of about 213 feet across a small town, so storm runoff has somewhere to go, and it often goes through basement walls on the way.

Clear gutters after the leaves fall and aim downspouts away from the house. A basement wall that weeps during a fall storm is a sign to grade the soil away from the foundation. Walk the property during rain and tell us which wall is wet.

Rebuilding once the structure is dry

Drying comes first, then rebuilding. After the readings are back to normal, water damage repair replaces drywall, insulation, flooring and paint. If framing, wiring or plumbing is involved, ask the Maynard building department about permits before work begins.

Cost follows the water category, the affected area, the materials, the drying days and equipment, and the rebuild scope. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, so check your policy. Flood usually needs separate coverage. Call the 24/7 line for a real number.

Before the crew gets to your Maynard home

Whatever the season, these steps keep a small problem from growing.

Stop the flow

Shut the main valve. After a freeze, open the lowest tap to let trapped water drain out of the line.

Switch off wet circuits

Turn off breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry. If the basement is flooded, leave the panel alone.

Lift and shelter

Take rugs, shoes, books and electronics off the floor. In a mill unit, move stock away from outside walls.

Keep clear of dirty water

River or drain water can carry contaminants. Keep children, pets and bare hands out of it.

Phone the 24/7 line

Say what season of problem it is: freeze, river, humidity or storm. It helps us bring the right gear.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Maynard?

Maynard has one ZIP, 01754, and we cover all of it. That means downtown on Main and Nason Streets, the Presidential district on the south side, and the streets around Summer Hill.

Is emergency water removal available in Maynard?

Yes. The 24/7 line answers day and night in every season. We dispatch from Sudbury and discuss timing, access and equipment on the phone.

How fast does the Assabet River rise?

It can rise quickly in a snowmelt or storm event. The river dropped about 30 feet across town and reached about 2,500 cubic feet per second in March 2010. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer about flood coverage.

What do I do when pipes freeze in an old house?

Shut the main, open the nearest faucet, and call us. Do not use an open flame to thaw pipes. Once the line thaws, we check walls and ceilings for hidden moisture.

Can the mill buildings get mold in summer?

Any building can when humidity or a leak keeps materials wet. Mold needs moisture, so we locate and dry the source. Ask any company whether state or local rules apply to mold work, and take health questions to a physician.

Do big masonry mill buildings take longer to dry?

Yes. Thick brick and heavy timber hold moisture deep inside, so we plan extra readings and extra days of drying equipment. The Assabet Woolen Mill complex, now Mill & Main Place, is the kind of building where that matters.

Do you also serve Stow and Acton?

We serve Stow (01775) and other nearby towns. The Assabet also crosses the northwest corner of Marlborough, and our water mitigation team follows the same drying process wherever the call comes from.
