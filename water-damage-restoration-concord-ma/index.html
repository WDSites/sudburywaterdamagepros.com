Where two rivers become one

The Sudbury and Assabet rivers meet near Concord's town center to form the Concord River. It flows north to the Merrimack at Lowell. The place was first called Musketaquid, and the town was incorporated on September 12, 1635.

For a homeowner, the confluence means water arrives from two directions, and low ground near either river can stay wet after a storm upstream. The U.S. Geological Survey published a report on the Flood of March 1968 on the Sudbury, Assabet and Concord Rivers. Read it at the library or online if you want the history, and check the FEMA flood map for your address.

A flood policy is separate from a homeowners policy. Our flood damage restoration crew can describe what river water does to a building.

Septic or sewer: the question that shapes the cleanup

The Water and Sewer Division of Concord Public Works reports that its sewer system serves 1,692 customers, about 35 percent of the town, with roughly 33 miles of collection pipe. That leaves about 65 percent of Concord off town sewer, and many of those homes rely on septic systems.

If your toilet or floor drain backs up, tell us whether you have a septic tank and leach field or a town sewer connection. With septic, the cause may be a full tank, a saturated yard or a blocked line, and the fix may involve a septic contractor. Sewage is Category 3, so keep everyone out and never clean it yourself. Our water mitigation work begins once the source is controlled.

Town center and West Concord

The town's own water system draws from 6 groundwater wells and 1 surface supply, with two storage reservoirs holding 7.5 million gallons and about 121 miles of main. Town water serves about 95 percent of residents, with a small part of Carlisle and Acton included.

A leak inside your walls belongs to you, but a break in the street is the Water and Sewer Division's job. If your water suddenly turns cloudy or pressure drops on the whole block, check with neighbors and the division before you assume a pipe has failed in your own home.

West Concord is a census-designated place inside the town. The Concord Free Public Library keeps a building history of Anderson Market with a page on the Flood of 1936, which is a useful reminder that water has reached the village before. Neighbors in Maynard share the Assabet with you.

Great Meadows, Walden Pond and the woods between

Concord holds Walden Pond, Great Meadows National Wildlife Refuge, Estabrook Woods, Fairyland Pond, Egg Rock and Punkatasset Hill. Wetlands and ponds hold water near homes, and shaded, wooded lots dry slowly after rain.

Wet air lingers on shaded lots, so a house near that kind of ground can stay damp inside after a leak. Use a dehumidifier, open interior doors for airflow and check closets, behind furniture and under sinks every few days. Our crew also works south along the same river valley in Wayland.

Mold after the water: what the process looks like

Mold needs moisture. If a building stays wet, mold can follow, and drying fast is the best way to avoid it. Testing and remediation follow ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for the trade. Our mold remediation work includes containment barriers, HEPA air scrubbers and removal of affected porous materials.

We do not give health advice. Take health questions to your physician. We also never promise that mold will not come back, because only the moisture control around the building keeps it away. Ask any company whether state or local rules apply before you hire.

The bill follows five things: water category, area wet, materials, drying days with equipment, and rebuild scope. Insurers often pay for sudden and accidental leaks but often refuse gradual seepage, so read your policy or ask your agent. A real number takes one call to the 24/7 line.

Quick steps before we get to Concord

A calm first ten minutes can save flooring, furniture and a lot of drying time.

Name your water

Is it a supply line, a roof leak, a drain or a river? Stop what you can, starting with the main valve.

Protect the panel

Leave breakers on unless wet rooms can be switched off from dry ground. Never reach for the panel through water.

Set valuables high

Books, rugs and keepsakes belong on upper shelves. Wrap antiques in plastic and leave heavy pieces in place.

Keep drains and people clear

Do not flush or run taps if a drain backs up. Keep children and pets away from standing water.

Dial the 24/7 line

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Concord?

Concord has one ZIP, 01742, and we cover all of it. That includes the town center near the river confluence, West Concord, the Great Meadows side and the area around Walden Pond.

Is emergency water removal available in Concord?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line at any hour. Our crew leaves from Sudbury and we talk through access, water depth and equipment on the call.

Does it matter whether I am on septic or town sewer?

Yes. About 65 percent of Concord is not on town sewer. A septic backup can come from a full tank or a saturated yard, so we may need a septic contractor alongside our cleanup.

Who handles a broken water main in the street?

The Water and Sewer Division of Concord Public Works handles the public system. We handle damage inside your building, including extraction, drying and the rebuild afterward.

Is mold testing needed after every leak?

Not always. Many small leaks dry out before mold starts. If wet materials sat for days or you see staining or smell mustiness, ask for an assessment under S520, and ask any company what applies in your town.

What should I have ready when I call about a Concord leak?

Have your cross streets, the source of the water, the floors affected and whether you use septic or town sewer. A few photos help us plan equipment before we leave Sudbury.
