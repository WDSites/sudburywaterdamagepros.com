Emergency water removal means pumping standing water out of a building as fast as it can be done safely. If water is pooled on a floor in Sudbury, MA, call the 24/7 line now. While you wait, stop the source, close the main water valve, lift valuables off the floor and keep people and pets away from the water.

When standing water is an emergency

Any water deeper than a puddle that is not draining is a problem. After a few hours it soaks into drywall, subfloor and furniture, and Category 1 clean water begins to turn into Category 2 gray water as it sits.

Call right away if water covers a floor in a finished basement, if it reaches outlets, baseboard heaters or a furnace, or if it is rising. Call too if it came from a toilet, a drain or the ground outside, since those sources are not clean. Pump-out is followed by drying that follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, so moisture readings and not the calendar decide when equipment leaves.

Pumps and extraction units: what runs first

Deep water goes first. Submersible pumps sit in the lowest spot and push water through hose to a driveway or yard drain away from the foundation. In a basement with a foot of water, a single pump can lower the level in stages while we watch the walls and floor.

Once the floor shows, extraction units finish the job. Wands and squeegee heads pull water from carpet, pad, hardwood and tile, and a portable unit gets into rooms where a hose cannot run. Moisture meters then show how much water is still in the materials.

Water that goes into drains, outside, or into the yard is chosen based on what is in it. Sewage or oily water is handled differently from clean water, and we will say so before pumping starts.

Extraction is not drying

Getting the standing water out is only the first stage. A floor that looks wet-free can still hold water in the subfloor, the bottom of wall studs and the back of baseboard.

That moisture is removed by air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and monitoring, which is the work of water mitigation. Skipping it is how a musty smell or swollen trim shows up weeks later. We plan the handoff before the pumps shut off, so no time is lost between stages.

Burst pipe water damage in a New England winter

Freezing weather is the main reason pipes burst in New England. Water expands as it turns to ice, and pipes in unheated spaces like exterior walls, crawl spaces, garages and attics can split. The break often shows itself only when the ice thaws, and then water runs freely.

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons in a few hours. Close the main water valve first, then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain what is left in the lines. Turn off the water heater if it is in the affected area.

Once the supply is stopped, we pump standing water, extract from floors and remove ceiling or wall materials that are soaked. A plumber repairs the pipe, and we do not do plumbing repair. To avoid a second loss, keep heat on in cold weather, open cabinet doors under sinks on freezing nights and know where your main valve is. A burst-pipe loss also leads into water damage cleanup for the wet materials.

When water touches electrical panels or outlets

Water and electricity can kill. If water has reached an outlet, a power strip, an appliance or the electrical panel, do not stand in the water and do not touch any of them.

Switch off the breaker only when the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If you cannot, step outside and ring the utility or 911 before you phone us. The same goes for any gas smell, a hissing sound or a pilot light that is out.

Our crew checks for live circuits before wading in, and a licensed electrician should inspect wet wiring and the panel before power is restored.

What to do while you wait for the crew

These steps help keep the loss small while a crew is on the way. Skip any step that is unsafe.

  • Close the main water valve, or the shut-off for the broken fixture.
  • Move rugs, papers, electronics and small furniture to higher ground.
  • Put aluminum foil or plastic under furniture legs that stand in wet carpet.
  • Take photos and short video of the water and what it touched.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on water, and do not run fans through sewage.
  • Open interior doors so water can be reached, and keep children and pets away.

Where Sudbury water comes from and where to shut it off

The Sudbury Water District, created by an Act of the Massachusetts Legislature in 1934, is separate from the Town's public works department. Your main shut-off is inside your house, usually near where the pipe enters the basement or utility room.

If the leak is on the street side of your meter, call the Water District. If it is inside, close your own valve. Homes outside the central water area and septic homes along Route 20 and elsewhere have their own systems, so find your valve and write down its location today.

Rising groundwater and river or brook flooding add to the picture, and those jobs lean on flood damage restoration.

What affects the cost of water removal

Removal is priced by scope. A shallow spill on one floor and a flooded basement call for very different crews and equipment.

  • The water category, since gray and black water need protective gear and added sanitizing.
  • The depth and area of the water, which sets pump time and the number of extraction units.
  • The materials soaked, such as carpet, pad, hardwood or drywall.
  • How hard the area is to reach, including stairs, basements and crawl spaces.
  • Whether drying and rebuild follow, which are separate phases.

Removal and your insurance policy

Homeowners policies often respond to sudden events such as a pipe break or appliance failure. Slow leaks and neglect often are not covered, and outside flood water typically needs its own policy.

Read your policy or ask your insurer. Photograph the water before it is pumped and keep every receipt.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle emergency water removal emergencies at night or on weekends in Sudbury?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered nights, weekends and holidays. We do not promise an arrival time, but we will tell you what to do while you wait and send a crew when available.

How fast should standing water be removed?

As soon as it is safe. Water soaks into drywall and subfloor within hours, and clean water can turn to gray water as it sits.

Can I use a wet-dry vacuum to remove the water?

Only for small, clean spills and only if no power is at risk. For deep water or anything near outlets, wait for pumps and extraction units.

Is the job over once the water is out?

No. Hidden moisture remains in walls, subfloor and framing. Drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers follows removal.

What if my basement floods during a storm and the power is out?

Stay out of the water. Call the utility or 911 if you see downed lines, sparks or smell gas, then call the 24/7 line.

Do I have to shut off the water before you arrive?

If a pipe or fixture is the source, yes. Close your main valve if you can reach it safely. The Sudbury Water District handles problems on its side of the meter.
