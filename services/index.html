How the services fit together

A water loss moves through stages, and each stage has its own work. Standing water comes out first, drying starts immediately after, wet materials are removed and cleaned, and rebuilding comes last. Mold is a separate branch that can start when drying is late.

If you are not sure which stage you are in, call the 24/7 line. The dispatcher will sort it out by asking where the water came from, how long it has been wet and whether anything electrical is involved.

Start with the water that is still moving

When pipes are leaking, a basement is filling or a sump pump has quit, emergency water removal is the page to read. It covers pumps, extraction units and what to do while you wait, including burst pipes in a New England freeze.

River and storm flooding belongs on the flood damage restoration page, because that water is treated as contaminated. That page also covers basement flooding and sewage backups, which matter in a town where most homes are on septic.

Drying and cleaning once the water stops

Water mitigation is the 24 to 72 hour window when moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers decide how much of the building can be saved. The longer a wall stays wet, the more likely the next step is demolition.

Water damage cleanup takes over for the work on soaked materials, debris, sanitizing and odor. It is where the decision between drying a carpet and pulling it gets made, using the water category as the guide.

Rebuilding and mold

After drying, water damage repair rebuilds drywall, insulation, flooring and paint, and it covers ceiling collapses and cupped hardwood floors. You can use our crew or hand the dry house to your own contractor.

If a musty smell or dark patch has appeared, read about mold remediation. The key rule is that mold needs moisture, so the leak is fixed first and the containment and removal follow S520 practice.

What we do not do

Restoration is not waterproofing, foundation repair or septic repair. We dry and rebuild what water damaged, and we will tell you when a plumber, septic contractor or waterproofing company should be involved. We also do not promise insurance approval or an arrival time.

Frequently asked questions

Which service do I need first?

Whichever matches the water you can see. Moving water points to emergency removal, wet but stopped points to mitigation, and rebuild questions point to repair. The dispatcher can sort it out on the 24/7 line.

Do you serve towns outside Sudbury?

Yes. We serve Framingham, Wayland, Marlborough, Maynard and Concord, with pages for each. See the Framingham and Concord pages for examples of how local conditions differ.

Can I use my own contractor for the rebuild?

Yes. Many homeowners have us dry and clean up, then hand over a dry, documented house. Moisture records help your contractor start without guessing.

Is mold part of water damage work?

It is closely related. Mold needs moisture, so late or incomplete drying can lead to it. The mold remediation page explains containment, testing and when the leak must be fixed first.
